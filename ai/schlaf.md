### 问题

睡眠安排

> 「孩子到底该自己睡，还是跟爸爸妈妈睡？」

### 引言

一位家长觉得陪睡是常态，也是珍贵的亲密；另一位想到的是安全建议，以及大人也需要睡够。婴儿期这一层还多一个安全问题，不只是习惯问题。

### 场景

夜里十一点多，两岁半的 Nele 在小房间里第三次哭起来，Jonas 已经站在门口，手搭在门把手上。

「我就把她抱过来一会儿。」Jonas 压低声音说。

### 共同目标

- 两个人都希望孩子睡够，也希望大人睡够。
- 婴儿睡眠安全对两个人都是硬线，不是口味问题。
- 夜里安抚不等于惯坏。

### 什么条件下哪种做法成立

#### 1. 同房不同床，还是同床？

- A（成立条件）：关于第一年，官方有明确建议：仰睡、在父母卧室里用自己的婴儿床、用睡袋而不是被子、卧室内不吸烟。这一条两个人都可以依靠。
- B（成立条件）：想要亲近、想要夜间哺乳，这个愿望是真实的，应该说出来。可以找和安全建议并行的方式去满足它——这和绕过安全建议是两回事。

#### 2. 早点自己入睡，还是再陪一段？

- A（成立条件）：如果孩子已经能自己入睡，而大人的睡眠长期被拖垮，调整是一个正当议题。
- B（成立条件）：如果孩子需要较多亲近，家里也承受得住，陪睡并不构成伤害。

Für einen Elternteil ist gemeinsames Schlafen selbstverständlich und wertvoll. Der andere denkt an Sicherheitsempfehlungen und daran, dass auch die Erwachsenen Schlaf brauchen. Beim ersten Lebensjahr geht es zusätzlich um eine Sicherheitsfrage, nicht nur um Gewohnheiten.

### Schools of thought side by side

#### 1. Learning to fall asleep alone, step by step

- Core claim: Falling asleep is a skill a child can practise: put into its own bed drowsy but still awake, rather than rocked or held to sleep – that is how the Chinese health standard WS/T 579—2017 puts it.
- Representative book: Richard Ferber: “Solve Your Child’s Sleep Problems”, revised edition 2006 (we know only the publisher’s text, not his method in the book)
- Fits whom: Families whose child could already fall asleep alone, and where the adults’ sleep is lastingly suffering.

#### 2. Taking closeness at night seriously

- Core claim: James McKenna’s lab studies how sleeping environments respond to the needs of families, and calls its view of normal, healthy infant sleep a major corrective to more traditional western sleep models.
- Fits whom: Families whose child needs a lot of closeness and where both parents can carry accompanying the child to sleep. In the first year the BIÖG recommendation (own cot in the parents’ bedroom) remains the frame; questions about the risk of sudden infant death or about where the baby sleeps are for your midwife or paediatrician.

#### 3. Same room, own bed – build the rhythm first

- Core claim: In the first year the baby sleeps in its own cot in the parents’ room – so BIÖG and the Chinese standard – and for the toddler years the standard describes a gradual move to a small bed; bedtimes and routines are settled first, and the method follows from that.
- Fits whom: Families who do not want to commit to one direction right away and would rather adjust the room, the bed and the timetable step by step.

#### 1. Can a child learn to soothe itself?

- One side: Yes, falling asleep can be practised: the Chinese health standard explicitly names falling asleep alone as an ability to be fostered.
- The other side: It depends on the child: comforting at night is not spoiling, and every child needs a different amount of closeness, so one timetable does not fit all.
- Of the official texts we reproduce here, only the Chinese standard names falling asleep alone as a goal; whether and how it can be trained, we did not find there. We have no study specifically on self-soothing in our sources.

#### 2. Should a child be allowed to cry for a while at bedtime?

- One side: Those who stick to the plan say: a short cry is bearable if the child is fed, safe and a parent is nearby – otherwise every night is renegotiated.
- The other side: Those who go straight in say: a crying child is telling us it needs something, and comforting at night is not spoiling.
- In the official texts we reproduce here we found nothing on this, and our sources contain no study on how long a child may cry – this point is not covered by us.

#### 3. Are you arguing about the method – or about the rhythm underneath it?

- One side: Method first: a clear plan that holds for both makes nights predictable – without one, you renegotiate in the hallway at eleven.
- The other side: Rhythm first: first understand which phase the child’s sleep and body rhythm is in and how much closeness it needs; the method follows from that.
- We found no such order in the official texts. According to the publisher’s text, Ferber’s book first explains how sleep and body rhythms develop and then offers several solutions – we know only that publisher text, not the book.
