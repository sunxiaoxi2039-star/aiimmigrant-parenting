### 问题

要不要买有机

> 「给孩子一定要买 Bio 吗？还是说这主要只是更贵？」

### 引言

一位家长觉得入口的、贴身的东西值得更讲究；另一位想知道多花的钱到底买到了什么。这是预算与价值观的问题，不该被当成健康承诺来谈。

### 场景

冷藏柜前，岚把贴着绿色标志的有机牛奶放进购物篮，和每个星期一样。

雅各布瞄了一眼价签：「普通的也是牛奶啊。」「可她每天早上就喝这个，」岚说，手没有从纸盒上拿开。

米卡四岁，坐在购物车里，指着标签问「妈妈，哪一瓶是 gute Milch？」，两个人谁都没有马上回答。

### 一句话定位

> 争的不是有机「好不好」，而是多付的这笔钱到底在买什么——一种生产方式、更少的残留、一份安心，还是别的——以及同样的钱放在别处会不会更值。

### The question

Organic or not

> “Does it have to be organic for our child — or is it mainly just more expensive?”

### Introduction

One parent wants to look more closely at everything the child eats or wears on their skin. The other wants to know what exactly they are paying more for. This is a question of budget and values — and it should not be conducted as a health promise.

### The scene

At the dairy fridge, Lan puts the organic milk with the green label into the basket, the way she does every week.

Jakob glances at the price tag: “The regular one is milk too, you know.” “But it's the one she drinks every morning,” Lan says, keeping her hand on the carton.

Mika, four, sitting in the cart, points at the label and asks, “Mama, which one is the good 牛奶?” – and neither of them answers right away.

### What this is really about

> The argument is not whether organic is “good” but what the extra money actually buys – a way of producing food, fewer residues, peace of mind, or something else – and whether the same money would do more elsewhere.

### Two perspectives (no winner)

### Real disputes (not adjudicated)

#### 1. For the child's body – or for the way food is produced?

- One side: Buying organic can simply mean approving of how the food is produced – organic production, species-appropriate husbandry, yearly inspections – without needing a health result in the child to justify it.
- The other side: When it is about the child, the first question is what changes for the child's body; if that cannot be named, I do not want to pay more for it.
- The official pages describe the label as a mark of production and inspection; they say neither that a child must eat organic nor that it is a waste of money. Our source collection has nothing on long-term health outcomes of organic food for children.

#### 2. The same money: the label – or more vegetables and fruit?

- One side: If you care about residues and have the budget, you take the same vegetables with the label.
- The other side: If you have to count the money, you would rather buy more and more varied vegetables and fruit for the same amount; the label comes second.
- The comparison “organic or more vegetables and fruit for the same money – which is better for the child?” is not covered by our source collection, and the official pages give no result on it either.

#### 3. Is the label a promise of safety?

- One side: There are rules and inspections behind the label – that is enough for me, I do not need to check every product myself.
- The other side: The label only says how something was produced; it does not promise “safer” or “healthier”, so I still look for myself.
- The texts we read (BMLEH, the Chinese certification measures) are about who may say “organic” and how inspections work; no health promise appears in them. BZfE writes only for tomatoes: “not healthier as a matter of principle”, though according to studies they often contain more nutrients.
