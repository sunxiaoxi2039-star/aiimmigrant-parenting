家里的中文

### 引言

对一位家长，中文连着祖辈和自己的成长经历；另一位在意压力，希望聊天能轻松一点。在寻找语言规则之前，先把各自的在意说出来。

### 场景

刚过八点，莉娅（5 岁）躺在床上，刚讲完绘本里的小刺猬怎么把冬天睡过去了——用德语讲的，两只手臂还张得开开的。

兰坐到床边，用中文说：「明天外婆要来视频，你把小刺猬讲给她听，好不好？」莉娅把被子拉到下巴，想了想：「可是用德语讲更快。」她不是顶嘴，她只是困了。

门口站着托比亚斯，抱着一筐衣服，小声问：「你们刚才说什么？」兰翻译给他听。屋里静了一会儿，两个人心里其实是同一个问题：这个晚上，到底该属于哪种语言？

### 流派并排

#### 1. 一人一语

- 主张：每位家长始终用自己的语言和孩子说话，并遵守清楚的、尽量始终照做的语言规则。
- 适用谁：两位家长各自有把握说不同的语言，而且每天都和孩子聊得比较多的家庭。

#### 2. 在家全家说较弱的那种语言

- 主张：在家里，尽量全家人都说在外面处于弱势的那种语言；德语孩子本来就会在家庭之外学到。
- 代表书：Grosjean《Bilingual: Life and Reality》（哈佛大学出版社，2010）——我们只读了他的网页
- 适用谁：两位大人在家都能、也愿意说中文的家庭。

#### 3. 先保住开口的乐趣

- 主张：让孩子愿意开口：聊天不变成语言考试，孩子用德语回答或混着说，也不必每次都纠正。
- 适用谁：用中文聊天容易变得费劲，或者觉得轻松比纠正更重要的家庭。

### Die Frage

Chinesisch zu Hause

> „Ich spreche Chinesisch, unser Kind antwortet auf Deutsch. Muss ich es korrigieren — oder darf das Gespräch einfach weitergehen?“

### Einführung

Für einen Elternteil steckt in Chinesisch die Nähe zu Großeltern und zur eigenen Geschichte. Der andere sorgt sich um Druck und darum, dass Gespräche leicht bleiben. Es hilft, diese Anliegen auszusprechen, bevor ihr eine Sprachregel sucht.

### Die Szene

Kurz nach acht: Lia (5) liegt im Bett und hat gerade erzählt, wie der Igel aus ihrem Bilderbuch den Winter verschlafen hat – auf Deutsch, mit beiden Armen.

Lan setzt sich auf die Bettkante und sagt auf Chinesisch: „Morgen ruft Waipo an. Erzählst du ihr vom Igel?“ Lia zieht die Decke bis zum Kinn, überlegt und antwortet: „Aber auf Deutsch geht es schneller.“ Sie meint es nicht frech, sie ist müde.

### Reading notes from well-known authors

_Reading notes are editorial summaries of one author's view. They are not official guidance and not verified research evidence. Always name the author and the book when citing._

#### François Grosjean (psycholinguist, University of Neuchâtel) — "Bilingual: Life and Reality", Harvard University Press 2010 – we read his own web page on myths about bilingualism

- He calls it a myth that bilingualism delays children's language acquisition.
- In his account the home language does not harm the school language; it can serve as a base for acquiring the other one.
- For him the critical factor is need: the child must sense that both languages are needed in daily life. “One person – one language” often breaks down there; he suggests that, where possible, everyone at home uses the weaker language.
- Limits: We read his web page, not the book. The page summarises and does not cite individual studies there. BIÖG words it slightly differently: when several languages are acquired at once, language development may at first be somewhat delayed. Both statements are shown side by side here.
- What we read: [Grosjean: “Myths about bilingualism” (own website)](https://www.francoisgrosjean.ch/myths_en.html)

#### Annick De Houwer (linguist, director of HaBilNet) — Harmonious Bilingualism Network (HaBilNet) – we read its page for families

- She writes that there is no overall language delay in young bilingual children; at least one language develops at the level expected for the child's age.
- Reasons to pay attention: no words in any language by one and a half, or no little sentences in any language soon after the second birthday. In her account the bilingual setting is never the cause.
- “Harmonious bilingualism” in her sense is about how well the family feels with its languages – not only how well the child speaks.
- Limits: These are texts from a network that aims to support bilingualism – not a neutral review. We read the website, not her books. Her own 2007 survey study is listed in the evidence above.
- What we read: [HaBilNet: “For families”](https://www.habilnet.org/for-families/)
- What we read: [HaBilNet: “What is HaBilNet?”](https://www.habilnet.org/what-is-habilnet/)

### Pre-written demo Q&A (not a live AI, not evidence)

- Question: I speak Chinese with our daughter (5); she always answers in German. My husband says I shouldn't push her. Should I insist that she answers in Chinese?
- Answer: The sources collected here say nothing about “insisting”. What BIÖG does say: even if the child speaks only German for a phase, parents should always answer and speak in their own, familiar language – so you can stay with Chinese without interrupting the conversation. BIÖG also writes that, especially at school age, the mother tongue is at risk of withering; the child needs chances to use both languages meaningfully. A survey of 1,899 families found the minority language was more likely to last where both parents used it at home. Your husband raises a different point – that talking should not turn into a test. The two fit together: you stay with Chinese, and together you look for a situation where your daughter really needs Chinese, such as a call with her grandparents. This is a collection of public guidance, not advice for your child.
